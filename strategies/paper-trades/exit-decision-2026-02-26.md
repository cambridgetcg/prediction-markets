## Strategy Lessons

### T003 (S004 Contrarian)
- **Lesson T11**: Not all elevated pricing is "overreaction" — sustained crisis pricing behaves differently from event-driven spikes. The S004 playbook trigger criteria (≥10% in 24h or ≥20% in 7d) weren't cleanly met for T003. We used S004 logic on what was really a gradual crisis premium. This should be classified differently in future.
- **Lesson T12**: Near-term and medium-term market segments can move in opposite directions — the Feb 28 market dropped while the Mar 31 market rose. This means our thesis can be directionally correct (near-term panic was overblown) without making money on a longer-dated position.

### T004 (S003 Catalyst)
- **Lesson T13**: Cross-market divergence edges can close in the wrong direction. The CME-Poly gap at 3.05pp suggested Polymarket was too pessimistic — but CME dropped to meet Polymarket, not the other way around. The more sophisticated market (CME) was the one that was wrong, not Polymarket. **Prediction markets can be more efficient than traditional derivatives on tail probabilities.**
- **Lesson T14**: Asymmetric lottery tickets require constant re-evaluation of the probability estimate, not just the payoff ratio. A 35:1 payoff is meaningless if your probability estimate drops below 1/35 (2.86%). The moment PCE came in hot, we should have re-evaluated immediately rather than waiting.
- **Lesson T15**: S003 early entry (23 days before catalyst vs. 3-7 day ideal) increases exposure to thesis-killing data arriving before the catalyst. Future S003 entries should stick closer to the 3-7 day window. If the Feb 20 PCE had come out AFTER we entered (e.g., entering Mar 11), we would have caught it and avoided entry.
