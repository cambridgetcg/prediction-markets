## Executive Summary

Maduro was captured by US Delta Force on January 3, 2026 (Operation Absolute Resolve). He's in Brooklyn Metropolitan Detention Center facing narco-terrorism charges with a mandatory 20-year minimum. Vice President Delcy Rodriguez assumed acting presidency backed by the military. The Chavista institutional apparatus remains intact below her. The opposition (Machado in exile in Miami/Oslo, Gonzalez in Spain) has no path to power without elections or US-imposed regime change — neither of which is imminent.

The dominant scenario: Rodriguez cooperates just enough with the US on oil access and token prisoner releases, keeps power, and the Chavista structure endures through 2026. The US appears content with oil access + Maduro in custody.

## 2. Current Power Structure

### De Facto Government

| Official | Role | Power Base | Notes |
|----------|------|------------|-------|
| **Delcy Rodriguez** | Acting President (sworn Jan 5) | Civilian Chavista faction; brother Jorge controls National Assembly | First woman head of state in Venezuela. Slow-walking reforms. |
| **Diosdado Cabello** | Interior Minister | Police forces + armed colectivos (paramilitaries) | Most powerful indicted official still in Venezuela. US warned he could be targeted. |
| **Vladimir Padrino Lopez** | Defense Minister (since 2014) | Military (FANB) | Also indicted on narco-terrorism charges ($15M reward). Activated state of emergency. |
| **Jorge Rodriguez** | President of National Assembly | Legislative control | Delcy's brother; swore her in |

### Rodriguez's Governing Approach (Feb 2026)

- **Slow-walking reforms**: Doing "just enough to look as if they're complying" (NPR)
- **Amnesty law** (Feb 19): 379 political prisoners granted amnesty on paper, but only **16 verified releases** as of Feb 22 per Foro Penal; released detainees banned from speaking to press, leaving country, or political activity
- **Oil reform bill**: Gives foreign companies greater rights over Venezuela's oil
- **NOT discussed**: New elections or judiciary reforms
- Claims Maduro remains "the legitimate president of Venezuela"

### Power Dynamics

Three factions in uneasy alliance:
1. **Civilian Chavista** (Rodriguez siblings): Pragmatic survival, minimal concessions to US
2. **Military-security** (Cabello + colectivos): Hardline, controls street-level enforcement
3. **Formal military** (Padrino Lopez + FANB): Institutional, recognized Rodriguez

Growing tensions between factions over level of cooperation with US demands.

Sources: NPR, CNN, Bloomberg, Al Jazeera

| Peru | Fujimori | Fled to Japan amid scandal | 2000 | No |
| Ecuador | Mahuad | Military-backed removal | 2000 | No |
| Bolivia | Morales | Resigned after disputed election + military pressure | 2019 | Indirect |

### Closest Parallel: Panama 1989

**Noriega captured → legitimate winner (Endara) installed → democratic transition within months.**

Key differences from Venezuela:
1. Panama had a clearly recognized election winner (Endara) who was physically in the country and immediately installed
2. The US dissolved the Panamanian military entirely
3. Panama had no parallel institutional structure (no equivalent of Chavista TSJ, NA, etc.)

In Venezuela: The legitimate winner (Gonzalez) is in exile. The US has NOT installed him. The institutional structure remains Chavista. The military was not dissolved. This means the transition is **much slower and less certain** than Panama.

### Post-External-Intervention Transition Timelines

| Country | Intervention | Leader Removed | Transition to Elections | Total Time |
|---------|-------------|----------------|----------------------|------------|
| Panama (1989) | US invasion | Noriega captured | Elections held May 1989 (pre-invasion) | Immediate — winner installed |
| Iraq (2003) | US invasion | Saddam captured Dec 2003 | Elections Jan 2005 | ~22 months |
| Libya (2011) | NATO intervention | Gaddafi killed Oct 2011 | Elections Jun 2012 | ~8 months (but led to chaos) |
| Afghanistan (2001) | US invasion | Taliban removed | Elections Oct 2004 | ~36 months |

**Key pattern**: Even with full external intervention, elections take 8-36 months. In Venezuela, where the US has NOT demanded elections, the timeline is likely much longer.

## 9. Scenario Analysis

### Scenario 1: Rodriguez Status Quo (60-70% probability)

Rodriguez continues slow-walking reforms. US gets oil access. Token prisoner releases. No elections called. Chavista apparatus intact. She remains de jure and de facto leader through December 31, 2026.

**Required**: US continues to accept the deal. No internal Chavista coup. No dramatic escalation of civil unrest.

### Scenario 2: Chavista Internal Reshuffle (10-15% probability)

Cabello or a military faction challenges Rodriguez. Power struggle within Chavismo. Could lead to Cabello taking formal power, or a military junta, or a different Chavista figure. Does NOT lead to opposition taking power.

**Trigger**: US pressure on Cabello (indictment leverage), growing military discontent, Rodriguez perceived as too conciliatory.

### Scenario 3: Negotiated Transition (5-10% probability)

US or international pressure forces a negotiated transition process. Could involve:
- Transitional council with opposition participation
- Election scheduled for late 2026 or early 2027
- Machado or Gonzalez gains formal recognition

**Trigger**: US policy shift (Trump demands elections), international pressure, massive civil unrest that threatens oil production.

### Scenario 4: Machado Returns to Power (5-8% probability)

Machado returns to Venezuela (56% market probability), builds a mass movement, forces elections or de facto power transfer. Becomes recognized leader by end of 2026.

**Required**: Return to Venezuela + elections called + allowed to run + wins + takes office — all by Dec 31. Extremely compressed timeline even if everything goes right.

### Scenario 5: Maduro Somehow Returns (2-5% probability)

Legal case collapses or resolved through plea deal. Released and returns to Venezuela. Reassumes power.

**Required**: US releases him (why?), Venezuela accepts him back (Rodriguez is in power now), he reasserts control. Each step has low probability.

### Scenario 6: Gonzalez Assumes Presidency (3-5% probability)

International community increases pressure; Gonzalez recognized and installed as president.

**Required**: Major policy shift from multiple countries. US would need to demand this. Currently not on any agenda.

From February 2026, elections by ~August-September 2026 at the absolute earliest, with Machado assuming power by October-November at best.

**What would change the Machado estimate upward:**
