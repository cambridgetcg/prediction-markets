# GEO — Geopolitics & Conflict

## Scope
Wars, diplomacy, treaties, sanctions, territorial disputes, military action, regime change, international organizations.

## Mental Models
- **Deterrence theory** — states act on credible threats, not rhetoric alone
- **Base rate reasoning** — how often do threats become action historically?
- **Selectorate theory** — leaders act to stay in power; understand whose support they need
- **Escalation ladder** — conflicts follow steps; skip-steps are rare but catastrophic
- **Geographic constraints** — logistics, proximity, terrain shape what's possible
- **Domestic audience costs** — public statements constrain future options
- **Coercive diplomacy** — military buildup as negotiation leverage; distinguish "strike-ready" from "strike-imminent" by watching for evacuation orders and AUMF activity
- **Precedent momentum** — once a state has struck a target, the barrier to striking again drops dramatically (e.g., US struck Iran June 2025 → "round 2" discussion is qualitatively different from "first strike")
- **Deadline dynamics** — Trump sets deadlines; key question is whether he extends (as with North Korea in first term) or follows through (as with Iran 60-day deadline → June 2025 strikes)
- **Asymmetric retaliation** — Iran's deterrent is not symmetrical defense but multi-front escalation: missiles at Israel, Houthis on shipping, militia attacks on US forces, Hormuz disruption
- **Oil-as-constraint** — high oil prices directly undermine Trump's domestic agenda; Hormuz risk creates an economic ceiling on escalation willingness
