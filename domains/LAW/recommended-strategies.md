# LAW — Recommended Strategies

*The arcane edge. Legal domain expertise is rare and valuable.*

## Why LAW Has Hidden Edge

- **Legal reasoning ability is rare among prediction market participants.** Few traders read court filings, understand precedent, or can interpret regulatory comment periods. This creates a genuine, persistent information edge.
- **Court calendars are the most predictable catalysts in existence.** Oral argument dates, opinion release schedules, regulatory comment deadlines — all published and reliable.
- **Low-medium liquidity.** Niche markets, but growing as legal/regulatory questions gain attention (Trump deregulation, EU AI Act, crypto regulation).
- **Resolution is very clear.** Court rules or doesn't. Regulation passes or doesn't. Binary with minimal ambiguity.
- **Low volatility normally.** Spiky on ruling days — but by then it's resolution, not a tradable swing.

## Strategy Priority (ordered)

### 1. S003 News Catalyst — **PRIMARY** (Score: 5/5)

Court calendars are the gold standard of predictable catalysts.

**Why it works here**: Supreme Court oral arguments, circuit court opinion dates, regulatory comment deadlines, congressional committee votes — all on published calendars. The market doesn't always price in the timing correctly, especially for lower-profile cases.

**How to deploy**:
- Build LAW catalyst calendar: SCOTUS term calendar, major circuit cases, regulatory deadlines
- For each catalyst: assess which prediction markets are affected
- Enter 3-7 days before (5-14 days for regulatory deadlines, which often slip)
- Research: read the actual filings, understand precedent, assess judge/justice tendencies
- Exit: on ruling day or 48h post if market hasn't fully priced

### 2. S008 Information Edge — **PRIMARY** (Score: 4/5)

Legal domain expertise is one of the most durable edges.

**Why it works here**: Type A (synthesis) — reading briefs + amicus filings + oral argument transcripts + judge history. Type B (base rate) — historical reversal rates, regulatory approval rates, enforcement action patterns. Type C (model) — understanding legal doctrine gives a better mental model than "I think the court will..."

**How to deploy**:
- For each LAW market: read the primary source documents (filings, briefs, regulatory text)
- Form probability estimate from legal analysis BEFORE checking market
- Classify edge type
- Document in `LAW/research/`

### 3. S001 Hold to Resolution — **CALIBRATION** (Score: 4/5)

Legal calibration edge should be measurable.

**Why it works here**: If you can read court filings and understand precedent, your calibration on legal outcomes should exceed the market average. S001 tests this directly.

**How to deploy**:
- Standard S001 protocol
- Track Brier score for LAW specifically
- Compare performance: are legal markets your strongest domain?

## Strategies to Skip in LAW

- **S002 Threshold (2/5)**: Low volatility except on ruling days (which are resolution, not swings).
- **S005 Momentum (2/5)**: Legal outcomes are discrete. Regulatory shifts happen at specific moments, not trends.
- **S007 Arb (2/5)**: Mostly standalone binary markets. Limited connected outcomes.
- **S006 Time Decay (3/5)**: "Regulation by [date]" markets exist. Deadlines slip often, which favors NO. But market is often already efficient on this.

## The LAW Edge

**Read what nobody else reads.** The participants who trade LAW markets on Polymarket almost certainly haven't read the actual filings. If you do, you're the most informed person in the market. This is the purest form of S008-Type-A edge.
