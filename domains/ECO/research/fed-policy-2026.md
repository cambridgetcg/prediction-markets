- Current pause began: January 28, 2026
- Interest on reserve balances: 3.65%

## 2. Inflation Trajectory

### CPI (January 2026, released Feb 13)
([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/13/cpi-inflation-report-january-2026.html))

| Measure | January 2026 | Prior Month | Trend |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-------|
| Headline CPI YoY | 2.4% | 2.7% | Declining |
| Core CPI YoY | 2.5% | ~2.9% | **Slowest since March 2021** |

### PCE (December 2025, latest available)
([source](https://www.bea.gov/data/personal-consumption-expenditures-price-index))

| Measure | December 2025 | November 2025 | Trend |
|---------|--------------|---------------|-------|
| Core PCE YoY | 3.0% | 2.8% | **Reaccelerated** |

- January 2026 PCE releases **March 13, 2026** (5 days before March FOMC)
- Goldman Sachs estimates January core PCE at 0.40% MoM / 3.05% YoY — hot ([source](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/goldman-sachs-revises-pce-inflation-020300342.html))

### Services vs Goods
- Core goods inflation largely resolved
- Services inflation remains sticky, driven by shelter and insurance
- CPI is showing faster disinflation than PCE (divergence is notable)

### Shelter Inflation
([source](https://www.bostonfed.org/publications/current-policy-perspectives/2024/a-faster-convergence-of-shelter-prices-and-market-rent-implications-for-inflation.aspx))
- CPI shelter lags market rents by 8-14 months
- Forecast: shelter inflation ~2.5% annualized in H1 2026, ~2.1% in H2 2026
- Below-average new-tenant rent growth expected to reduce core CPI by ~37bp in 2026
- This is the key disinflationary force — but it's slow-moving

### Summary Assessment
**Mixed picture**: CPI looks encouraging (core at 2.5%), but core PCE reaccelerated to 3.0%. The CPI-PCE divergence matters because the Fed targets PCE. Shelter disinflation is coming but slowly. Tariffs are an upside risk to inflation.

([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/02/11/jobs-report-january-2026-.html))

| Indicator | January 2026 | Consensus | Prior |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|-------|
| NFP | +130K | +55K | Low (avg 15K/month in 2025) |
| Unemployment | 4.3% | 4.4% | 4.4% |
| Avg hourly earnings MoM | +0.4% | +0.3% | — |
| Avg hourly earnings YoY | +3.7% | +3.7% | — |

### Sector Detail
- **Gains**: Healthcare (+82K), social assistance (+42K), construction (+33K)
- **Losses**: Federal government (-34K, DOGE-related layoffs flowing through)
- Best payroll month since December 2024

### JOLTS (December 2025 data)
([source](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/jolts.htm))
- Job openings: **6.542M** — lowest since September 2020, well below 7.2M consensus
- Openings declined in professional/business services (-257K), retail (-195K), finance (-120K)
- Hires and separations: flat at ~5.3M each
- Quits: 3.2M (unchanged) — suggests workers less confident about switching

### Assessment
**Labor market is cooling but not collapsing**. Headline NFP beat was encouraging but the JOLTS data tells a clearer story — openings are drying up fast. The jobs/openings ratio continues normalizing. Wage growth at 3.7% is still above what's consistent with 2% inflation but trending in the right direction. The labor market supports the Fed's "patient" stance — no urgency to cut, but no reason to hike either.

## 4. Fed Chair Nomination — Kevin Warsh

### Background
([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/30/trump-nominates-kevin-warsh-for-federal-reserve-chair-to-succeed-jerome-powell.html))
- **Age**: 55
- **Experience**: Fed Governor 2006-2011 (Bush appointee), previously at Morgan Stanley and White House (National Economic Council)
- **Nomination date**: January 30, 2026
- **Powell's term ends**: May 2026
- **Other finalists**: Kevin Hassett (NEC), Christopher Waller (Fed Governor), Rick Rieder (BlackRock)

### Policy Views
([source](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/what-trumps-nomination-of-inflation-hawk-kevin-warsh-means-for-the-federal-reserve))
- Historically labeled an **inflation hawk** and **balance sheet hawk**
- Strongly opposed QE during his Fed tenure — viewed it as emergency-only tool
- **Recent evolution**: now favors lower interest rates in public statements
- Key philosophical difference from Powell: shift from "model dependence" to "results-oriented" policy
- Favors combination of QT (to rebuild credibility) + rate cuts (to stimulate)
- Would likely reduce reliance on dot plots and forward guidance in favor of flexibility
- CFR assessment: "won't revolutionize the Federal Reserve" — markets reassured by his experience ([source](https://www.cfr.org/articles/why-kevin-warsh-wont-revolutionize-the-federal-reserve))

### Senate Confirmation Dynamics
([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/30/fed-warsh-tillis-trump-powell-doj.html))
- **Key blocker**: Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC) — refuses to vote yes until DOJ drops criminal probe into Powell
- Senate Banking Committee: 13R-11D — Tillis voting no creates a tie, blocks committee recommendation
- Tillis is retiring (term ends Jan 2027) — less susceptible to party pressure
- Tillis won't accept deal where DOJ drops probe in exchange for Powell leaving — demands full resolution
- Treasury Secretary Bessent says Senate will proceed with hearings ([source](https://katv.com/news/nation-world/senate-will-hold-confirmation-hearings-for-federal-reserve-chair-nominee-kevin-warsh-treasurys-scott-bessent-says-thom-tillis-doj-jerome-powell))
- If DOJ probe resolved, confirmation expected to succeed

### Polymarket
- "Who will Trump nominate as Fed Chair?" — Warsh at **95%**, Judy Shelton at 3%
- "Kevin Warsh formally nominated by...?" — March 31 at 84%
- "Warsh confirmed as Fed Chair by May 15?" — active market
- Total volume on nomination market: $508.8M

### Key Uncertainty
The binding constraint is **not** whether Warsh will be confirmed (expected yes) but **when** — the Tillis/DOJ drama could delay confirmation past Powell's May term expiration, creating an acting Chair scenario.

## 7. Trump Tariff Impact

### Current Tariff Landscape (as of Feb 23, 2026)

**Major developments**:
1. **SCOTUS ruling (Feb 20)**: 6-3 decision struck down IEEPA tariff authority — all IEEPA tariffs are invalid ([source](https://taxfoundation.org/blog/supreme-court-trump-tariffs-ruling/))
2. **Trump response**: Announced new 15% global tariff (up from initial 10%) under alternative authority
3. **Section 232 tariffs remain**: 25% on imported cars from most countries still in effect
4. **Greenland tariffs**: Paused after NATO framework deal in January ([source](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/trump-tariffs-nato-greenland-davos.html))

**Effective tariff rate**: ~16.9% overall (highest since 1932) ([source](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/state-us-tariffs-february-20-2026))

### Inflation Impact
([source](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/federal/trump-tariffs-trade-war/))
- Goldman: tariffs added +0.5pp to inflation in 2025, expected +0.3pp in H1 2026
- Consumer pass-through accelerating: businesses absorbed ~80% of costs in 2025, expected to pass through 55-70% in 2026 as inventories deplete
- Atlanta Fed's Bostic: 40% of unit cost growth in 2025-2026 attributable to tariffs
- Fed Vice Chair Jefferson: "lack of progress" on inflation target "appears to be due to tariff effects"
- Section 232 tariffs alone: $635B revenue over next decade, ~$400/household in 2026

### Fed Constraint
Tariffs create a **stagflation dilemma**:
- Inflationary (pushes prices up) → argues against cutting
- Growth-dampening (reduces economic activity) → argues for cutting
- The Fed's stated position: treat tariff-driven price increases as "transitory" initially, but monitor second-round effects on expectations
- **Net effect**: tariffs are the primary reason the Fed is on hold despite cooling labor market

### SCOTUS Ruling Impact
The SCOTUS ruling creates massive legal uncertainty. Key questions:
- Will the 15% replacement tariff survive legal challenge?
- Does Congress need to act to authorize tariffs?
- Markets saw initial relief but Trump's escalation to 15% reversed gains
- This is a rapidly evolving situation that could materially change the Fed's calculus

## 8. Historical Comparison — Fed Pauses

### Current Pause Context
- Cutting cycle: Sep 2025 → Dec 2025 (three consecutive 25bp cuts, total 75bp)
- Pause began: January 2026
- Duration so far: ~1 month

### Historical Averages
([source](https://www.lpl.com/research/blog/sector-returns-during-a-fed-pause.html))
- **Average pause duration**: 146 days (~5 months)
- **Recent pauses (last 6)**: 240 days (~8 months)
- **Longest**: 2006-07 pause at 446 days
- **Average time from last hike to first cut**: ~10 months

### Relevant Comparisons
| Period | Context | Pause Length | Next Move |
|--------|---------|-------------|-----------|
| 2006-07 | Housing bubble → financial crisis | 446 days | Emergency cuts |
| 2019 | Mid-cycle insurance cuts, then pause | ~5 months | More cuts (COVID) |
| 2023-24 | Post-hike plateau, inflation sticky | ~13 months | Began cutting Sep 2024 |

### Assessment
The current environment most resembles a **mid-cycle pause with inflation constraints** — similar to 2019 but with added tariff uncertainty. If the 240-day recent average holds, the pause would extend through approximately **September 2026** before the next cut. This aligns with market pricing of a June-September cut window.

What would trigger the next move:
- **Cut**: Sustained labor market deterioration (unemployment above 4.5%), clear disinflation in PCE
- **Hike**: (very unlikely) Inflation re-acceleration above 3.5% with rising expectations
- **Emergency cut**: Financial market stress event, recession signal (inverted yield curve deepening)

## Assessment: Are Polymarket Prices Fair?

### Fed March Decision (96% no change)
**Verdict: FAIR** — Possibly slightly overpriced by 1-2pp given the two FOMC dissents and cooling labor data, but not enough to trade against. The CME FedWatch at 94.1% is the more informative market. No edge here.

### Warsh at 94% (for nomination)
**Verdict: SLIGHTLY OVERPRICED** — Warsh has already been nominated (Jan 30). The question is really about confirmation timing, not whether he'll be the nominee. The Tillis blockade is real: a retiring senator with no party loyalty incentive. If the DOJ probe isn't resolved before Powell's May term expiration, there's a scenario where:
- Warsh gets stuck in committee (5-10% probability)
- An acting Chair serves temporarily
- This doesn't change the eventual outcome but creates volatility

The 95% Polymarket price for Warsh as nominee is fair since the nomination happened. The more interesting market is **confirmation timing** — "confirmed by May 15" has meaningful uncertainty.

### Most Likely Surprise Scenario
**A more dovish March dot plot than expected.** The two dissents at January FOMC, the JOLTS collapse, and the SCOTUS tariff ruling reducing tariff overhang could lead several dots to shift down. If the median moves from 3.4% to 3.15% (implying two cuts), this would significantly reprice rate expectations and be bullish for risk assets. Probability: ~20%.

**Secondary surprise**: Warsh confirmation fails or is severely delayed. If Tillis holds and other GOP senators waver, this could create Fed leadership uncertainty. Probability: ~8%.
