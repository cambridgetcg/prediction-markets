# ECO — Recommended Strategies

*The most efficient domain. Respect the machine — trade structure, not conviction.*

## Why ECO Is Hard

- **Kalshi has a perfect forecast record** on Fed rate decisions (2022-June 2024). You are competing against Bloomberg consensus, Fed funds futures, and professional rates traders.
- **Resolution is extremely clear.** No ambiguity in "Fed holds at 5.25%." This is why it's efficient — clear criteria attract smart money.
- **Information digestion is near-instantaneous.** Within seconds of data releases, prices adjust. Speed traders extract $4.2M aggregate. You can't beat them on speed.
- **Only 0.51% of Polymarket wallets profit >$1,000.** In ECO, that number is probably lower.

## Strategy Priority (ordered)

### 1. S003 News Catalyst — **PRIMARY** (Score: 5/5)

The one reliable edge: understanding the asymmetry of scheduled events.

**Why it works here**: FOMC meetings, CPI releases, jobs reports, GDP prints — all on a known schedule. The edge isn't predicting the outcome. It's understanding the *asymmetry of possible outcomes* and whether the market has correctly priced the tail scenarios.

**How to deploy**:
- Build an ECO catalyst calendar (FOMC dates, CPI release dates, jobs Fridays)
- 3-7 days before each: assess the market pricing vs consensus
- Where is the market diverging from futures/Bloomberg? That divergence is either your edge or your warning.
- Enter only when you have a specific thesis for why the consensus is wrong
- Size conservatively (3% max) — you're betting against professionals

**Best opportunities**: Surprise data. When CPI comes in hot/cold vs expectations, secondary markets (recession probability, tariff markets) may be slow to reprice.

### 2. S006 Time Decay — **SELECTIVE** (Score: 3/5)

Occasional edge when retail misunderstands rate path probability.

**Why it works here (selectively)**: "Fed cuts by March" markets attract retail participants who don't understand the difference between "Fed will eventually cut" and "Fed will cut by this specific meeting." When the market implies a cut probability much higher than Fed Funds futures, the time premium is overpriced.

**How to deploy**:
- Compare Polymarket Fed pricing to CME FedWatch
- If Polymarket implies significantly higher cut probability than futures → buy NO
- Hold to resolution
- Only enter when the gap is ≥5% (covers fees and accounts for platform differences)

**Watch for**: Polymarket and CME price different things. Make sure you're comparing apples to apples.

### 3. S007 Cross-Market Arb — **SELECTIVE** (Score: 3/5)

Structural inconsistencies between related rate path markets.

**Why it works here**: "Fed cuts in March" vs "Fed cuts by June" vs "Fed holds all year" should be logically consistent. Nested timeline markets in ECO sometimes diverge.

**How to deploy**:
- Check temporal consistency: "cuts by March" ≤ "cuts by June"
- Check mutual exclusivity: all FOMC meeting outcomes should sum to ~100%
- If inconsistency ≥3%, enter both sides

## Strategies to Avoid in ECO

- **S001 Hold (2/5)**: Kalshi is *perfect*. Your calibration edge is near-zero against this field.
- **S004 Contrarian (2/5)**: Professional participants don't overreact to data. They process it in seconds.
- **S005 Momentum (2/5)**: Fed decisions are discrete events. Rate paths are well-telegraphed. No slow-digestion momentum.
- **S002 Threshold (2/5)**: Low volatility. Thresholds rarely trigger.
- **S008 Info Edge (3/5)**: High info density but zero effort barrier. Bloomberg terminal owners already know everything you could research.

## The ECO Rule

**Trade structure, not conviction.** In ECO, your conviction about the outcome is worthless — the market is too efficient. Instead, trade *structural features*: catalyst timing (S003), time premium overpricing (S006), and logical inconsistencies (S007). These edges exist independent of your ability to predict the economy.
