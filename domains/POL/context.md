## Scope
Elections, legislation, executive appointments, party dynamics, polling, governance.

## Mental Models
- **Fundamentals vs narratives** — economy, incumbency, demographics matter more than news cycles
- **The Party Decides (modified)** — party elites still shape primaries, but less than pre-2016
- **Median voter theorem** — general elections pull toward center
- **Approval ratings** — strongest single predictor of incumbent party performance
- **Institutional constraints** — what a leader WANTS vs what they CAN DO
- **Primary dynamics** — crowded fields, lane theory, momentum effects

## 2028 Presidential Election Models

### VP Succession Model
- Sitting VPs win party nomination ~80% of the time (8/10 since 1960)
- Sitting VPs win the general election ~14% of the time (1/7 since 1960)
- Only HW Bush (1988) succeeded as sitting VP since Van Buren (1836)
- Third-term fatigue is real: power alternates in ~8yr cycles since 1952

### Economic Fundamentals Model
- GDP growth > 3.2% in election year → incumbent party usually wins
- Current forecast: ~1.8% for 2028 — below threshold but not recessionary
- Misery Index (inflation + unemployment) correct in 13/15 elections
- Economy explains ~12% of results but may be declining in importance due to polarization

### Early Frontrunner Predictiveness (Mayer Threshold)
- Frontrunners above 34% in Sept before election year win nomination 6/8 times
- Frontrunners below 34% win 0/5 times
- Vance at 53% is well above threshold → strong nomination favorite
- 2.5 years out, markets are unreliable: 2006 Clinton lost to Obama, 2014 Trump wasn't in picture

### Out-of-Power Party Selection Patterns
- After a loss, parties oscillate between establishment and insurgent nominees
- 2004: establishment (Kerry), 2008: insurgent (Obama), 2012: establishment (Romney), 2016: insurgent (Trump), 2020: establishment (Biden)
- The losing party typically corrects for perceived weaknesses of its last nominee
- After Harris (establishment, unfocused), Dems likely prioritize clear economic messaging

### Demographic Structural Trends
- Education polarization: 13-point college gap in 2024, widening
- Non-college voters now 57% of electorate → structural GOP advantage in Electoral College
- Latino vote shifting R since 2016 (Trump won 47% of Latino men in 2024)
- Black turnout declining since 2012 peak; young Black men at 25% turnout
- Gen Z fully voting age by 2028 (~52M+ eligible) but volatile — not locked into either party

### Electoral College Math
- Democrats need PA+MI+WI (Rust Belt path, ~42 EV) — Sun Belt alone can't reach 270
- Blue states losing EVs via reapportionment; red/purple states gaining
- Trump won all 7 swing states in 2024 (margins: WI +0.9% to AZ +5.5%)

## European Mental Models
- **Front republicain decay** — far-right second-round firewall declining each cycle; below 55% = RN wins
- **Minority government survival** — fragmented opposition ≠ stable government; depends on whether opposition can unify on a single no-confidence motion
- **Polling overestimation of RN** — systematic across 7 recent French election rounds; Bardella's 35-37% may be inflated
- **Institutional incumbency advantage** — Orban controls state media/courts; Starmer has 400+ seat majority. Structural protection > poll numbers.
- **EU regulatory hostility to prediction markets** — Dutch ban, broader EU crackdown. Reduces European trader participation → creates informational asymmetry favoring informed observers trading from elsewhere.
- **Three-bloc paralysis** — France, and increasingly Germany, face parliaments where no bloc can govern alone. This produces weak minority governments and frequent collapses.
