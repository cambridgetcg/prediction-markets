# 2028 US Presidential Election — Research Report

*Compiled: February 23, 2026*
*Sources: Web search across major political outlets, polling aggregators, prediction markets, academic research*

---

## Executive Summary

The 2028 presidential race is structurally a wide-open contest despite JD Vance's dominance of the Republican primary. Key findings:

- **Vance is ~80% likely to win the GOP nomination** (historical VP base rate + 53% primary polling + Trump near-endorsement), but only ~20-25% likely to win the presidency (historical VP general election failure rate + net-unfavorable ratings + third-term fatigue)
- **The Democratic field is genuinely open** — Newsom leads markets at ~28-30% but is vulnerable on California's record; Harris leads polls at ~39% but carries "lost already" baggage; no one has formally announced
- **Structural factors favor the challenger party** — third-term fatigue, tepid GDP growth (~1.8%), education polarization, and Electoral College reapportionment all create mild-to-moderate headwinds for Republicans
- **The biggest value opportunities** are on the Democratic side, where the market's uncertainty creates the most mispricing potential

## 1. Republican Field

### J.D. Vance — The Prohibitive Favorite (41-48% on Polymarket for nomination)

**Current Position**: Sitting VP, RNC finance chair, Trump's near-endorsed successor.

**Approval**: 42% favorable / 53% unfavorable overall (Civiqs, Feb 2026). **91% favorable among Republicans** (Dec 2025). Deeply underwater with independents and Democrats.

**Key Strengths**:
- Institutional lock: RNC finance chair, MAGA infrastructure, Turning Point USA endorsement
- Trump has called him "most likely" heir (Aug 2025); Trump's "dream ticket" is Vance-Rubio
- Fundraising: Silicon Valley + MAGA donor pipelines. One Nantucket event raised $5M+
- Venezuela/Maduro operation involvement gives foreign policy credentials
- Youth (41 in 2028) — generational figure
- 91% GOP favorability — no rival close

**Key Weaknesses**:
- Chained to Trump's record (Trump approval 39-42%, Feb 2026)
- Net-unfavorable with general public (-9 to -11)
- Only 28% of non-MAGA Trump voters would follow a Trump endorsement
- Turning Point antisemitism controversy (Dec 2025) — vulnerability with moderates
- Munich Security Conference speech (Feb 2025) — alienated allies, isolationism attack surface
- Polymarket presidential odds declining: 31% → 23-24% (late 2025 to Feb 2026)

## 2. Democratic Field

### Why So Open?

1. **2024 was a decisive loss**: Trump 312 EV vs Harris 226 EV. Trump won all 7 swing states and the popular vote (49.8%).
2. **Harris underperformed Biden by 6.8M votes** in 45 of 50 states
3. **No incumbent or heir**: Fully out of power (no WH, no Congress)
4. **Identity crisis**: Deep split over progressive vs moderate direction
5. **Governors busy governing**: Top candidates (Whitmer, Newsom, Shapiro, Pritzker, Beshear) all in office through 2026-2027
6. **2024 autopsy suppressed**: DNC completed internal report but won't release it

### Top Candidates

#### Gavin Newsom (~28-30% in markets) — EARLY FRONTRUNNER
- CA governor, term ends Jan 2027. Book tour, podcast, NH appearance March 2026
- **Strengths**: Highest name recognition; viral anti-Trump brand; massive donor network (Soros, Hoffman, Bloomberg); $3.9M in leadership PAC
- **Weaknesses**: California record is devastating — homelessness (187K), $1M median home price, LA fires, insurance crisis, bullet train. "California liberal" label in the general. Nate Silver says "if electability is the priority, it should be Beshear, not Newsom"
- **Assessment**: Strong primary candidate, potentially weak general election candidate

#### Kamala Harris (~8% in markets, ~39% in polls) — THE REMATCH QUESTION
- On book tour ("107 Days"); told BBC she could "possibly" still be president; relaunched Kamala HQ
- **Strengths**: Highest poll numbers (39%); 90% favorable among Dem likely voters; massive name ID and infrastructure; beats Newsom 56-44 head-to-head
- **Weaknesses**: She already lost. $1.15B campaign failed. Represents Biden-era continuity voters rejected. Many potential candidates and donors want someone new.
- **Assessment**: If she runs, she's the polling frontrunner but divisive within the party

#### Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (~9.5% in markets) — PROGRESSIVE LANE
- Positioning for president or NY Senate; Munich Security Conference appearance fueled speculation
- **Strengths**: Sanders coalition heir; enormous small-dollar fundraising potential; massive social media presence; Nate Silver called her a top contender; polled as beating Vance in hypothetical
- **Weaknesses**: Never held statewide office; progressive candidates typically can't assemble majority coalition; electability concerns in swing states; only 35 in 2028
- **Assessment**: Strong in the primary progressive lane but faces a ceiling

#### Josh Shapiro (~6.1% in markets) — THE ELECTABILITY PLAY
- PA governor, running for reelection 2026; 60% job approval (Quinnipiac, Oct 2025)
- **Strengths**: Governs the most important swing state; leads Vance 53-43 in PA hypothetical; actively recruiting Congressional candidates and remaking PA Democratic Party; strong law-and-order credentials
- **Weaknesses**: Passed over as Harris VP pick in 2024; lower national name recognition; Jewish identity cuts both ways given Israel/Palestine dynamics
- **Assessment**: Strongest electability argument in the field. Underpriced.

#### Pete Buttigieg (~4.6% in markets) — NH FRONTRUNNER
- Former Transportation Secretary; moved to Michigan; holding Iowa town halls
- **Strengths**: Leads NH polls at 19-20%; exceptional communicator; only 44; 2020 Iowa winner
- **Weaknesses**: Biggest race won was mayor of South Bend (100K pop); tied to Biden admin; no current office
- **Assessment**: Could break out via NH win, like he did via Iowa in 2020

#### Andy Beshear (~4.2% in markets) — RED-STATE DEMOCRAT
- KY governor, term ends late 2027; chair of Democratic Governors Association
- **Strengths**: Won 3 statewide races in a state Trump carried by 26 points. Nate Silver says he's "Mr. Electability, not Newsom." Authentic, relatable.
- **Weaknesses**: 20% name recognition among Dems (12th place). Late decision timeline. Small-state fundraising base. May "read as too centrist" for primary.
- **Assessment**: Strongest general election candidate but needs a breakout moment. Underpriced.

#### JB Pritzker (~4% in markets) — BILLIONAIRE POPULIST
- IL governor, 2nd term; $3.9B net worth; 100+ national media interviews in 2025
- **Strengths**: Unlimited self-funding ($350M+ already spent on campaigns); progressive governing record; aggressive Trump antagonist
- **Weaknesses**: Wealth is an obstacle in a populist era; Hyatt fortune = "out of touch"; IL baggage (pensions, crime, population flight); deep blue state doesn't prove swing appeal
- **Assessment**: Could outlast others financially; needs the field to thin

#### Gretchen Whitmer (~3.4% in markets) — FADING?
- MI governor, term-limited Jan 2027; reported to lack "fire in the belly"
- Multiple reports of retreating from national stage since late 2025
- Strong governing record and swing-state credentials, but Michigan shifted to Trump in 2024
- **Assessment**: Declining probability. Markets may be overpricing based on 2023-2024 name recognition.

#### Other Notable Names
- **Cory Booker**: 25-hour filibuster speech went viral; now polling 11% in some surveys
- **Mark Kelly**: AZ senator; raised $33M since 2022; astronaut/military credentials; "seriously considering"
- **Wes Moore**: Explicitly ruled out 2028 on multiple occasions
- **Raphael Warnock**: Up for Senate reelection 2028; strategic conflict
- **Jon Ossoff**: GA senator; Nate Silver says "acceptable to all three factions"

### Democratic Party Dynamics

**Three factions**:
1. **Progressive populists** (AOC, Sanders wing): Economic populism, Medicare for All
2. **Center-left institutionalists** (Shapiro, Beshear, Buttigieg): Electability, pragmatism
3. **Anti-Trump fighters** (Newsom, Pritzker): Confrontation-first, ideologically flexible

**Emerging consensus**: Economic populism works, cultural progressivism doesn't. Sanders: Democrats lost because they relied on "race, gender, and sexual orientation rather than raising the minimum wage."

**Primary calendar in flux**: DNC selecting 4-5 early states from 12 applicants (Iowa, NH, SC, NV, MI, GA, and others). Calendar composition will significantly affect candidate viability.

## 3. Structural Factors

### Third-Term Fatigue
- Since 1952, power alternates in ~8-year cycles with only two exceptions
- After two terms, incumbent party "no longer wields any advantage" (Cambridge UP)
- **Direction: Headwind for GOP. Confidence: High.**

### Economic Conditions
- GDP growth forecast: ~1.8% for 2027-2028 (CBO, Morgan Stanley, Deloitte)
- Below the 3.2% "incumbent party wins" threshold but not recessionary
- SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariffs (Feb 20, 2026); admin pivoting to Section 122
- Recession probability: ~30% (RSM)
- **Direction: Mild headwind for GOP. Confidence: Medium.**

### Demographics
- **Gen Z**: Fully voting age by 2028; ~52M+ eligible. Volatile — not locked into either party. 2024 showed rightward shift among young men.
- **Latino vote**: Shifting R (Trump won 47% of Latino men in 2024). May partially reverse.
- **Black turnout**: Declining since 2012 peak; young Black men at 25% turnout — structural Dem weakness.
- **Education polarization**: 13-point college gap in 2024, widening. Non-college voters now 57% of electorate. Structurally benefits GOP in Electoral College.

### Electoral College Map
- Trump swept all 7 swing states in 2024 (margins: WI +0.9% to AZ +5.5%)
- Reapportionment: Blue states losing EVs; red/purple states gaining
- Democrat needs Rust Belt (PA+MI+WI = ~42 EV) — Sun Belt alone can't reach 270
- **Direction: Slight advantage GOP. Confidence: Medium-High.**

### Historical Base Rates — Prediction Markets 2.5 Years Out
- **Feb 2006 for 2008**: Clinton was dominant frontrunner; Obama invisible → Clinton LOST nomination
- **Feb 2014 for 2016**: Trump not in the picture at all → Trump WON nomination
- **Feb 2018 for 2020**: Biden in the picture but far from locked → Biden WON but nearly collapsed
- **Feb 2022 for 2024**: Trump dominant early → Trump WON (exception: incumbent-like status)

**Key threshold** (Mayer research): Frontrunners above 34% in Sept before election year win nomination 6/8 times. Vance at 53% is well above this. Harris at 39% is above it but in a much less settled field.

**Takeaway**: 2.5 years out, the GOP nomination is relatively predictable (Vance), but the Democratic nomination is highly uncertain. Markets are appropriately uncertain on the Dem side.

## 4. Value Opportunities

### Where Is the Market Most Likely Wrong?

**Assessment Framework**: Compare market prices to my independent estimate, identify gaps > 5 percentage points.

#### GOP Nomination
- **Vance at 41-48%**: Probably fair to slightly underpriced. Historical VP nomination rate (80%) + 53% primary polling + Trump's tilt suggests 55-65% is more accurate. The 41% Polymarket price may reflect general election doubt bleeding into nomination pricing.
- **Rubio at 14-19%**: Probably overpriced if he truly won't run when Vance does. But correctly priced as insurance against Vance collapse.
- **DeSantis at 3-5%**: Roughly fair. Damaged goods but not zero.

#### Dem Nomination
- **Newsom at 28-30%**: Probably overpriced by ~5-10pp. California baggage is severe; Nate Silver and others question his electability; market may be pricing name recognition more than viability. Fair value: ~18-22%.
- **Harris at 8% (markets) vs 39% (polls)**: One of these is very wrong. Markets are discounting her heavily because she lost, but polling shows massive Dem voter support. If she runs, she's probably 25-30% to win the nomination. If she doesn't run, 0%. The market is pricing in significant probability she doesn't run, which is reasonable. Still, 8% seems low — fair value: ~12-18%.
- **Shapiro at 6%**: Underpriced. 60% approval in the most important swing state. Nate Silver flags him as a top electoral performer. If he runs aggressively after 2026 reelection, he's a serious contender. Fair value: ~10-15%.
- **Beshear at 4.2%**: Underpriced. Won 3 races in Trump+26 state. DGA chairmanship builds relationships. "Mr. Electability" per Nate Silver. Main risk is low name recognition and late entry. Fair value: ~6-10%.
- **AOC at 9.5%**: Roughly fair. Strong progressive lane but ceiling problem. Could go up if she consolidates left early.
- **Buttigieg at 4.6%**: Slightly underpriced. NH polling lead at 20% is significant. Fair value: ~6-8%.
- **Mark Kelly**: Underpriced at current levels. Astronaut, swing state, $33M raised, compelling biography. If he enters, he's immediately a top-5 contender. Fair value: ~4-7%.

#### Presidential Winner
- **Vance at 23-24%**: Roughly fair to slightly overpriced. VP general election base rate is 14%; the economic/structural environment is mildly unfavorable. But he has real advantages (youth, fundraising, party unity). Fair value: ~18-22%.
- **Generic Democrat**: The "will a Democrat win?" question implies ~40-45% probability in the market. Given structural factors (third-term fatigue, tepid economy, education polarization), I'd put this at **45-55%** — the challenger party has a real edge.

## 5. Top 3 Value Bets

### 1. Josh Shapiro for Democratic Nominee — BUY at 6%
**My estimate: 10-15%**

Rationale: Governs the single most important swing state. 60% approval. Actively building 2028 infrastructure (recruiting candidates, remaking PA party, lining up donors). If 2026 midterms go well in PA, he has the strongest "I can beat Vance" argument. Nate Silver identifies him as a top electoral performer. The market is underweighting electability-focused candidates because the Democratic primary electorate historically rewards energy/excitement over electability — but the 2024 loss may have permanently shifted that calculus.

**Risk**: Low name recognition nationally. Passed over as VP pick. Jewish identity adds complexity. May not run.

### 2. Andy Beshear for Democratic Nominee — BUY at 4.2%
**My estimate: 6-10%**

Rationale: Won 3 elections in a state Trump carried by 26 points. Chair of the Democratic Governors Association (builds national relationships). Nate Silver's "Mr. Electability." Authentic, relatable, economically populist. If Democrats internalize the lesson from 2024 that they need someone who can win swing voters, Beshear fits perfectly.

**Risk**: Very low name recognition (20% among Dems). Late decision timeline (post-2027). Small state, limited fundraising base. May be "too centrist" for the primary.

### 3. Gavin Newsom for Democratic Nominee — SELL at 28-30%
**My estimate: 18-22%**

Rationale: California's record is a severe liability that the market is underweighting: homelessness crisis, $1M home prices, LA fires, insurance collapse, bullet train debacle, refinery closures. "California liberal" label will be weaponized by opponents in both the primary (electability argument) and general. The Intercept: "The Biggest Problem for Gavin Newsom's 2028 Run Is Gavin Newsom." The market is pricing his name recognition and media presence but not the negative ads that will define him once the campaign actually starts.

**Risk**: Newsom has massive fundraising and donor advantages. Could lock in endorsements early. If no strong alternative consolidates, he wins by default.

## Uncertainty Disclosure

**High confidence**:
- Vance is the strong GOP frontrunner and ~80% likely to win the nomination
- Historical structural factors (third-term fatigue, VP general election record) work against him
- The Democratic field is genuinely open with no locked-in frontrunner

**Medium confidence**:
- Shapiro and Beshear are underpriced as Democratic nominees
- Newsom is overpriced as Democratic nominee
- The economy will be a mild headwind for the GOP

**Low confidence**:
- Specific probability estimates for individual candidates (too many unknowns this far out)
- Whether Harris runs again (changes everything if she does/doesn't)
- Which "lane" wins the Democratic primary (progressive vs moderate vs fighter)
- Trump's behavior and its impact on the 2028 race

**What I don't know**:
- Who announces when (timing of entries dramatically reshapes the race)
- What the 2026 midterm results will be (the single most important catalyst)
- Whether a recession occurs
- Whether a currently unknown candidate emerges (cf. Obama 2006, Trump 2014)
