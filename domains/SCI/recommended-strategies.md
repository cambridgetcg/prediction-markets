# SCI — Recommended Strategies

*The expert's paradise with a liquidity problem. Massive potential edge, tiny exploitable market.*

## Why SCI Is Special

- **Highest potential information edge of any domain.** Scientific literacy is extremely rare among prediction market participants. If you understand clinical trial design, Nobel committee patterns, or replication crisis dynamics, you have a structural advantage.
- **Academic validation**: A landmark study showed prediction markets effectively predicted which psychology studies would replicate, outperforming traditional surveys. The edge is documented in the literature.
- **But liquidity is abysmal.** Polymarket has minimal SCI coverage. Position sizes are tiny. Metaculus is better for this domain but doesn't offer monetary returns.
- **Very low volatility.** Markets barely move. Scientific progress is gradual. Position capital sits idle.

## Strategy Priority (ordered)

### 1. S008 Information Edge — **PRIMARY** (Score: 5/5)

This is where S008 shines brightest.

**Why it works here**: Type B (base rate) — knowing historical Nobel patterns, clinical trial success rates, mission success rates. Type A (synthesis) — combining latest research papers + conference proceedings + institutional signals. The effort barrier is insurmountable for most participants — they don't read the papers.

**How to deploy**:
- For each SCI market: do the domain research that nobody else will
- Nobel predictions: historical committee patterns, citation analysis, field rotation
- Clinical trials: understand phase success rates, endpoint design, comparator choices
- Space milestones: mission timelines, historical delay rates, engineering constraints
- Enter on divergence ≥10%, hold to resolution
- Size at 3% max (liquidity constraint)

### 2. S001 Hold to Resolution — **CALIBRATION** (Score: 4/5)

Excellent calibration testing domain due to high potential edge.

**Why it works here**: Your scientific calibration vs the market should be measurably better here than in any other domain. Use SCI as a calibration proving ground. If your Brier score doesn't beat the market in SCI, it won't beat it anywhere.

**How to deploy**:
- Standard S001 protocol
- Track Brier score obsessively
- Compare your performance in SCI vs other domains as a diagnostic tool

### 3. S003 News Catalyst — **SELECTIVE** (Score: 3/5)

Known but sparse catalyst calendar.

**Why it works here**: Nobel Prize announcements (October), major conference presentations (NeurIPS, AAAS), clinical trial readout dates, space launch windows — all known in advance.

**How to deploy**:
- Build a SCI catalyst calendar (sparser than other domains)
- Enter only when a specific catalyst has a market with sufficient liquidity
- Conservative sizing

## Strategies to Avoid in SCI

- **S002 Threshold (1/5)**: No volatility. Markets don't move. Thresholds never trigger. Capital is trapped.
- **S005 Momentum (1/5)**: No trends. Scientific progress doesn't create tradable momentum.
- **S004 Contrarian (2/5)**: No spikes to fade. Market too thin for overreaction patterns.
- **S007 Arb (2/5)**: Too few connected markets. No arb surface.
- **S006 Time Decay (3/5)**: "Mission by [date]" markets exist. Base rates are knowable. But liquidity limits make it impractical.

## The SCI Strategy

**Use Metaculus for calibration practice. Use Polymarket only for high-conviction, liquidity-available SCI positions.**

SCI's real value isn't monetary returns — it's calibration training. If you can predict scientific outcomes well, that skill transfers to other domains. The domain expertise you build in SCI feeds S008 in other contexts.
