# Participant Psychology Profiles by Domain

*Research date: 2026-02-25*
*Purpose: Understanding WHO is on the other side of your trade reveals WHICH biases they're subject to*
*Prerequisite: behavioral-foundations.md (B01)*

---

## Overview

Each domain attracts a different psychological profile of bettor. The predictability of their biases is our edge. This document profiles all 10 domains.

**Cross-domain insight**: Edge is inversely proportional to domain sophistication. The domains with the most casual, emotionally-driven participants (GEO, CRY) produce the largest and most predictable mispricings. The domains with the most professional participants (ECO, FIN) produce near-zero exploitable emotion.

## GEO (Geopolitics) — The Fear Factory

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Doom-scrollers / news junkies** | ~40% | Following breaking news, entertainment, feeling "in the know" | Availability (#1), Recency (#16), FOMO (#10), Narrative (#4) |
| **Geopolitical hobbyists** | ~25% | Interest in international affairs, testing their analysis | Narrative (#4), Representativeness (#5), Confirmation (#20) |
| **Crypto-native speculators** | ~20% | High-risk tolerance, already on Polymarket for CRY, cross-trading | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25), Hope Premium (#13) |
| **Military/policy analysts (rare)** | ~5% | Genuine domain expertise, testing forecasts | Anchoring (#21), minimal bias if trained |
| **Insiders (govt, diplomatic, intelligence)** | ~10% | Informational edge, possibly hedging | Creates extreme asymmetry — they KNOW things we can't know |

### Primary Motivation

Following the news and feeling like you're "predicting" global events. GEO markets are the prediction market equivalent of doom-scrolling — emotionally engaging, anxiety-producing, and not optimized for accuracy.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Availability Heuristic** (#1) — Vivid war imagery, carrier deployments, satellite photos make strikes feel imminent regardless of base rates.
2. **Fear Premium** (#12) — The consequence of being wrong on "strike happens" (feeling stupid for holding NO while bombs drop) psychologically outweighs the mathematical loss of paying inflated YES prices.
3. **YES Bias** (#23) — "Will the US strike Iran?" is framed as an affirmative question. Betting YES = "something dramatic happens" = action. Betting NO = "nothing happens" = boring.
4. **Compound Probability Neglect** (#26) — A strike requires multiple conditions (political will AND military readiness AND trigger event AND failure of diplomacy). Traders price the narrative ("strike is coming") rather than multiplying the component probabilities.
5. **Narrative Fallacy** (#4) — "Escalation ladder" stories write themselves. Each new headline fits the narrative of inevitable conflict.

### Information Asymmetry

**Extreme.** Government insiders, military analysts, diplomatic sources know far more than retail. Evidence of insider trading: the Iran strike markets showed unusual volume patterns hours before the June 2025 Operation Midnight Hammer was announced. The question is not whether insiders trade — it's whether they're buying or selling, and whether they're loud enough to move prices before the information becomes public.

Our position in the asymmetry: We can't compete with insiders on private information. But we can compete on SYNTHESIS of public information (OSINT, diplomatic calendars, force posture analysis). Most retail traders don't synthesize — they react to headlines. Our edge is the gap between headline reaction and synthesized assessment.

### Emotional Trigger Map

| Trigger | Emotion | Buying Behavior | Typical Overpricing |
|---------|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| Military imagery (carriers, jets, troops) | Dread, anxiety | Rush to buy YES | +5-15pp |
| Leader quotes ("bad things will happen") | Fear, urgency | FOMO-driven YES buying | +10-25pp |
| Diplomatic talks announcement | Mild relief | Slow NO buying, minimal | -2-5pp (underreaction) |
| Confirmed strike | Shock → "what next?" | Secondary market YES spike | +15-30pp on secondary |
| Quiet day (no news) | Boredom, impatience | Drift — some YES holders sell | -0.5-1pp per quiet day |

### Our Edge

- Base rate analysis (threats ≫ action historically)
- OSINT synthesis (force posture, diplomatic signals, satellite imagery)
- Distinction between "rhetoric" and "genuine escalation indicators"
- Patience — willing to hold NO through headline storms
- The S006 + S004 + S008 stack operates on three timescales simultaneously

### Psychological Edge Score: **9/10**

Highest exploitable domain. Fear-driven retail participants, extreme info asymmetry, large systematic biases, growing liquidity.

## POL (Politics) — The Partisan Arena

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Political junkies / partisans** | ~35% | Tribalism, wanting to be right about "their" candidate | Motivated Reasoning (#7), Identity-Protective (#8), Confirmation (#20) |
| **Data-driven analysts (538-style)** | ~25% | Calibration testing, analytical hobby | Anchoring (#21), Conservatism (#19) — fewer biases overall |
| **Campaign insiders / pollsters** | ~10% | Genuine edge, hedging | Minimal bias — they're the smart money |
| **Crypto-native cross-traders** | ~20% | Already on platform, drawn by high-volume political markets | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25), Narrative (#4) |
| **Casual bettors (event-driven)** | ~10% | Debate nights, election day excitement | FOMO (#10), Recency (#16) |

### Primary Motivation

Identity validation. Political bets are identity statements masquerading as probability estimates. When you buy Trump YES at $0.65, you're not just estimating 65% — you're declaring tribal affiliation. This makes political markets simultaneously the most liquid (identity-driven volume) and the most biased (identity distorts probability assessment).

### Dominant Biases

1. **Motivated Reasoning** (#7) — Clinton & Huang (2025): 58% of Polymarket national markets show negative serial correlation, consistent with partisan-motivated buying creating spikes that reverse.
2. **Identity-Protective Cognition** (#8) — Kahan (2017): MORE numerate/literate individuals are MORE polarized. The sophisticated political traders are not less biased — they're more effectively biased.
3. **Confirmation Bias** (#20) — Traders selectively consume media that confirms their candidate's chances.
4. **Representativeness** (#5) — "My candidate had a strong debate" → "My candidate will win."
5. **Anchoring** (#21) — Current polling average becomes THE anchor, even when structural factors (incumbency, economy) matter more.

### Information Asymmetry

**Moderate.** Public information is abundant (polls, ratings, models). The edge comes from:
- Polling internals (crosstabs, likely voter screens) that most traders don't read
- Historical analogues that most traders don't know
- Base rate reasoning (VP nomination rates, incumbent advantages) that most traders don't apply
- Structural factors (22nd Amendment, party rules) that most traders ignore

### Emotional Trigger Map

| Trigger | Emotion | Buying Behavior | Typical Overpricing |
|---------|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| Major poll (candidate up 5+) | Validation/anxiety | Partisan buying surge | +3-8pp |
| Debate "knockout" moment | Excitement/despair | FOMO on winning candidate | +5-15pp |
| Scandal/legal action | Outrage/denial | Split: opponents buy, supporters don't sell | +5-15pp (short-term) |
| Endorsement by major figure | Social proof activation | Herding toward endorsed candidate | +3-8pp |
| Election night returns | Maximum intensity | Volume spike, spreads widen | Variable — efficient by close |

### Our Edge

- Base rate anchoring (VP nomination rate = 100%, 22nd Amendment = constitutionally binding)
- Independent estimate formation before checking market price
- Willingness to bet against partisan consensus
- Cross-referencing multiple polling aggregators rather than single polls

### Psychological Edge Score: **6/10**

Moderate. Sophisticated participants compete, but partisan bias creates persistent mispricings on identity-charged markets. The best edges are on structural factors (constitutional constraints, base rates) that partisan thinking ignores.

## ECO (Economics) — The Professional's Domain

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Fed watchers / rates traders** | ~40% | Professional overlap with futures/bonds | Anchoring (#21), Conservatism (#19) — trained to minimize bias |
| **Economists / analysts** | ~20% | Calibration testing, intellectual | Minimal systematic bias |
| **Informed retail** | ~25% | Following financial news, testing predictions | Recency (#16), Anchoring (#21) |
| **Casual cross-traders** | ~15% | Crypto-native, following macro for crypto correlation | Narrative (#4), Representativeness (#5) |

### Primary Motivation

Professional forecasting and calibration testing. ECO attracts the most analytically trained participants because the data is structured, scheduled, and professionally analyzed.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Recency Bias** (#16) — Even professional forecasters anchor to recent data. Campbell & Sharpe (2009): 25% of macroeconomic "surprises" are predictable from anchoring to recent values.
2. **Conservatism** (#19) — Market underreacts to genuine trend shifts (dovish → hawkish pivot takes weeks to fully price).
3. **Anchoring** (#21) — Dot plot, Bloomberg consensus, and previous FOMC statement anchor expectations.

**What's notably ABSENT**: Fear premium, YES bias, FOMO, herding, narrative fallacy. The professional participant base suppresses these biases. This is why S004 doesn't work in ECO.

### Information Asymmetry

**Minimal.** Bloomberg terminals, Fed Funds futures, economic calendars — all the same information is available to everyone. The marginal edge is:
- Speed (8 seconds after Powell speaks, contracts move 13% — speed trading)
- Interpretation of nuanced Fed language that retail misreads
- Cross-platform arbitrage (CME vs Polymarket) which is compressed

### Our Edge

**Near-zero.** We learned this with T004 (-17.5%). The CME-Poly divergence we spotted wasn't a mispricing we could exploit — it was a legitimate difference in participant interpretation, and the PROFESSIONAL market (CME) adjusted to match the RETAIL market (Polymarket), not vice versa.

### Psychological Edge Score: **1/10**

Lowest exploitable domain. Professional participants, efficient pricing, minimal emotional distortion. Only trade here with S003 (known catalyst timing).

## FIN (Financial Markets) — The TradFi Mirror

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **TradFi professionals** | ~35% | Overlap with equity/options trading | Minimal — they're the smart money |
| **Quant traders / algo runners** | ~20% | Systematic strategies, arb against underlying | Near-zero bias |
| **Retail equity traders** | ~30% | Cross-platform, already track markets | Recency (#16), Anchoring (#21), Representativeness (#5) |
| **Crypto-native cross-traders** | ~15% | General Polymarket users | Narrative (#4), YES bias (#23) |

### Primary Motivation

Overlap with traditional financial markets. Many FIN prediction market traders also trade the underlying equities/options and use prediction markets for expression of views they already hold.

### Dominant Biases

Minimal. The correlation with highly efficient equity markets means any bias is quickly arbitraged by participants who also trade the underlying. The one persistent bias:
- **Present Bias** (#17): Long-dated FIN markets (6+ months) suffer from the same present-bias discount as other domains.

### Our Edge

**Near-zero.** Any research edge in FIN is already priced in traditional financial markets first. Prediction markets are a lagging mirror.

### Psychological Edge Score: **1/10**

TradFi pros dominate. Not our terrain.

## CRY (Crypto) — The Emotional Rollercoaster

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Crypto-native retail** | ~50% | Already on Polymarket, high-risk tolerance, narrative-driven | FOMO (#10), Narrative (#4), Social Proof (#9), Hope Premium (#13), Identity-Protective (#8) |
| **DeFi degens** | ~20% | Yield farming, airdrop hunting, speculation | Action bias (#25), YES bias (#23), Probability Weighting (#14) |
| **Crypto influencer followers** | ~15% | Following calls from Twitter/YouTube personalities | Social Proof (#9), Herding (#9), Representativeness (#5) |
| **On-chain analysts** | ~10% | Genuine data edge (whale tracking, exchange flows) | Anchoring (#21) — but less biased overall |
| **TradFi crossover** | ~5% | Macro-crypto correlation traders | Conservatism (#19), Recency (#16) |

### Primary Motivation

**FOMO/FUD oscillation.** Crypto markets are driven by the most extreme emotional swings of any domain. The participant base has:
- High risk tolerance (accustomed to 30-50% drawdowns)
- Narrative-driven decision-making ("Bitcoin supercycle," "ETH ultrasound money")
- "Diamond hands" belief system that creates denial during drawdowns
- Identity attachment to specific tokens/ecosystems
- Musk effect: documented and quantified 5-20% price impact from tweets

### Dominant Biases

1. **FOMO** (#10) — The strongest single bias in CRY. Ante (2023): "particularly intensified in crypto-trading."
2. **Hope Premium** (#13) — Cheap contracts ($0.05-0.15 on "BTC to $200K") attract lottery-ticket buying.
3. **Probability Weighting** (#14) — 5% true probability weighted as 15% in decisions → overpriced longshots.
4. **Narrative Fallacy** (#4) — "Halving cycle," "institutional adoption," "crypto supercycle" — powerful narratives that override base rates.
5. **Identity-Protective Cognition** (#8) — Crypto community identity is as strong as partisan political identity. "Bitcoin maxis" resist negative information about BTC prospects.
6. **Social Proof / Herding** (#9) — Whale movements, influencer calls, and trending trades on CT (Crypto Twitter) create self-reinforcing cascades.

### Information Asymmetry

**Moderate but noisy.** On-chain data is public but requires infrastructure to analyze:
- Exchange inflow/outflow data (exchange reserve changes)
- Whale wallet tracking (large movements)
- Mining hashrate and difficulty adjustments
- ETF flow data (weekly, lagging)
- Options market data (implied volatility, put/call ratios)

Most retail traders DON'T analyze on-chain data. They react to CT narratives. The gap between on-chain reality and narrative pricing is the edge.

### Emotional Trigger Map

| Trigger | Emotion | Buying Behavior | Typical Overpricing |
|---------|---------|----------------|---------------------|
| Elon Musk tweet (bullish) | FOMO, excitement | Immediate buying cascade | +5-20pp |
| Protocol hack | Panic, FUD | Selling cascade across ALL crypto markets | +5-15pp on unrelated markets |
| ETF approval/inflow news | Euphoria | Narrative-driven buying ("institutional validation") | +3-10pp |
| Fed dovish pivot | Risk-on excitement | Broad-based buying | +3-8pp |
| "Extreme fear" index reading | Despair, capitulation | Selling stops, but contrarian buying begins | Opportunity zone for buyers |
| BTC round-number breach ($100K) | FOMO + narrative validation | Momentum buying | +5-15pp overshoot |

### Our Edge

- Base rate reasoning on halving cycle returns (not narrative-driven extrapolation)
- Understanding probability weighting to identify overpriced longshots
- Contrarian positioning when Fear & Greed Index reaches extremes
- Patience — willing to hold NO through "diamond hands" peer pressure

### Psychological Edge Score: **8/10**

Second-highest exploitable domain. Extremely emotional participant base, strong systematic biases (FOMO, hope premium, narrative), moderate liquidity. Only constraint: wash trading may inflate apparent volume.

## TEC (Technology & AI) — The Hype Cycle Domain

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Tech enthusiasts / AI followers** | ~40% | Following AI progress, testing predictions | Narrative (#4), Representativeness (#5), Anchoring (#21) |
| **AI industry insiders** | ~15% | Genuine domain expertise, calibration testing | Minimal systematic bias |
| **VCs / tech investors** | ~10% | Following the space for investment signals | FOMO (#10), Narrative (#4) |
| **Crypto-native cross-traders** | ~25% | General Polymarket users | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25) |
| **ML researchers** | ~10% | Testing forecasts on benchmark/model markets | Conservatism (#19) — trained but narrow |

### Primary Motivation

Excitement about technological progress, particularly AI. TEC markets attract a participant base that is more analytically capable than GEO/CRY but still susceptible to hype cycle dynamics.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Narrative Fallacy** (#4) — "Exponential progress" and "AGI is imminent" narratives override careful assessment of actual capabilities.
2. **Representativeness** (#5) — One benchmark improvement = general capability leap.
3. **Scope Insensitivity** (#3) — The difference between "GPT-4-level on benchmark X" and "GPT-4-level in deployment" is compressed.
4. **Anchoring** (#21) — Benchmark numbers become THE anchor for capability assessment.
5. **Hype Cycle dynamics** — Peak of Inflated Expectations creates systematic overpricing on new model markets; Trough of Disillusionment creates underpricing on adoption markets.

### Our Edge

- Following actual research (arxiv, benchmark methodology) vs. press releases
- Understanding that Arena ELO requires weeks of votes, not hours
- Historical pattern: announced "GPT-4 killers" have a ~20% rate of actually surpassing GPT-4 on Arena

### Psychological Edge Score: **5/10**

Moderate. More analytically capable participants than GEO/CRY, but hype cycle dynamics create periodic mispricings. Best exploited through S008 (domain knowledge) + S004 (fade hype peaks).

## SCI (Science & Health) — The Expert's Paradise (Liquidity Desert)

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Science enthusiasts** | ~40% | Interest in scientific progress, calibration testing | Representativeness (#5), Narrative (#4) |
| **Informed laypeople** | ~30% | Following pop-science coverage | Availability (#1), Scope Insensitivity (#3) |
| **Domain experts (rare)** | ~10% | Genuine expertise, testing forecasts | Minimal bias — but anchoring (#21) on prior knowledge |
| **Cross-traders** | ~20% | General Polymarket users, casual | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25) |

### Primary Motivation

Intellectual curiosity. SCI attracts a participant base that is generally more thoughtful but lacks the analytical intensity of ECO/FIN professionals.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Hope Premium** (#13) — "Will we cure X by date?" markets attract hopeful buyers.
2. **Representativeness** (#5) — "Promising trial result" → "cure is imminent."
3. **Availability** (#1) — Media-covered breakthroughs feel more likely than base rates warrant.
4. **Base Rate Neglect** — FDA approval rates, Nobel Prize base rates, and clinical trial success rates are publicly available but not applied.

### Our Edge

**Very high in theory, very low in practice.** Scientific domain knowledge is extremely rare among prediction market participants. But SCI markets on Polymarket are so thin ($1-50K volume) that we can't size into the edge. Metaculus is better for calibration practice on SCI questions.

### Psychological Edge Score: **7/10** (theoretical) / **2/10** (practical, due to liquidity)

The edge-to-liquidity ratio is inverted. Massive potential edge, minimal ability to exploit it.

## SOC (Society & Culture) — The Fun Money Zone

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Entertainment fans** | ~40% | Fun, following pop culture | Narrative (#4), Social Proof (#9), FOMO (#10) |
| **Sports bettors** | ~25% | Overlap from traditional sports betting | Representativeness (#5), Recency (#16) |
| **Casual Polymarket users** | ~25% | Browsing, entertainment | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25) |
| **Domain specialists** | ~10% | Industry insiders (gaming, entertainment) | Minimal bias — they know release schedules, production status |

### Primary Motivation

Entertainment. SOC markets are the "fun" side of Polymarket. Volume is moderate but biases are moderate too — the stakes feel low, so extreme emotions don't fire.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Social Proof** (#9) — "Everyone says Movie X will win the Oscar."
2. **Recency** (#16) — Latest rumor/trailer/review overwrites prior assessment.
3. **FOMO** (#10) — Trending markets attract attention.
4. **YES bias** (#23) — "Will Movie X break the box office record?" → people bet on exciting outcomes.

### Our Edge

Limited. Pop culture prediction is hard to systematize. The edge is narrow and comes from:
- Event calendars (release dates, award ceremonies are known)
- Industry insider knowledge (production delays, screening reactions)

### Psychological Edge Score: **3/10**

Low. Moderate biases but also moderate participant awareness. Not enough systematic mispricing to build a strategy around.

## LAW (Legal & Regulatory) — The Arcane Edge

### Who Bets Here?

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Legal hobbyists** | ~30% | Following high-profile cases | Narrative (#4), Availability (#1), Representativeness (#5) |
| **Partisan bettors** | ~25% | When legal outcomes have political valence (Trump cases, regulatory battles) | Motivated Reasoning (#7), Identity-Protective (#8), Confirmation (#20) |
| **Cross-traders** | ~25% | General Polymarket users | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25) |
| **Legal professionals (rare)** | ~10% | Genuine domain expertise | Conservatism (#19), Anchoring (#21) — trained minimizers |
| **Journalists / media watchers** | ~10% | Following legal news for reporting | Narrative (#4), Availability (#1) |

### Primary Motivation

High-profile legal dramas. LAW markets spike in attention around dramatic cases (Trump trials, tech antitrust, crypto regulation) and go dormant between events.

### Dominant Biases

1. **Narrative Fallacy** (#4) — Legal outcomes are complex and probabilistic, but media frames them as dramatic storylines ("the case that could bring down X").
2. **Availability** (#1) — High-profile cases get disproportionate coverage, inflating perceived likelihood of dramatic outcomes.
3. **Deadline Panic** (#18) — "Regulation by date" markets overestimate regulatory speed. Government regulatory timelines almost always slip.
4. **Identity-Protective Cognition** (#8) — When legal outcomes are partisan (Trump cases), identity bias dominates.

### Our Edge

- Court calendars are the most predictable catalyst structure in any domain
- Reading actual filings vs. media summaries (most traders read headlines)
- Understanding precedent and procedural requirements that constrain outcomes
- "Regulation by date" markets: government deadlines slip ~60% of the time → S006 edge

### Psychological Edge Score: **6/10**

Moderate-high. Domain expertise is rare among participants. The main constraint is liquidity (LAW markets are thinner than GEO/POL/CRY).

## ENV (Environment & Energy) — The Ghost Town

### Who Bets Here?

Almost nobody. ENV markets on Polymarket are extremely rare and extremely thin.

| Segment | % of Volume (est.) | Motivation | Bias Profile |
|---------|-------------------|-----------|--------------|
| **Climate enthusiasts** | ~40% | Environmental advocacy, testing predictions | Hope Premium (#13), Narrative (#4) |
| **Cross-traders** | ~50% | General Polymarket users, casual | YES bias (#23), Action bias (#25) |
| **Climate scientists (very rare)** | ~10% | Domain expertise | Conservatism (#19), Anchoring (#21) |

### Dominant Biases

1. **Hope Premium** (#13) — "Will we hit net zero by X?" markets attract hopeful climate activists.
2. **Narrative Fallacy** (#4) — Climate narrative either "everything is doomed" (overpricing disaster) or "technology will save us" (overpricing solutions).
3. **YES bias** (#23) — In the small sample of ENV markets, the general YES bias applies.

### Our Edge

Climate science literacy would be a genuine edge — but there are no liquid markets to exploit it on Polymarket. Metaculus is the better venue for ENV forecasting.

### Psychological Edge Score: **7/10** (theoretical) / **1/10** (practical, zero liquidity)

Skip entirely on Polymarket. Revisit if ENV market coverage expands.

### The Sophistication Gradient

```
Most Emotional ←————————————————————————→ Most Professional
    GEO   CRY   POL   LAW   SOC   TEC   SCI   ENV   FIN   ECO
   Edge: 9   8    6     6    3     5     2*    1*    1     1

* Liquidity-constrained: theoretical edge is much higher
```

### Key Insight

The gradient is not a spectrum — it's bimodal:
- **Exploitable** (GEO, CRY, POL): >60% retail/emotional participants. Systematic biases are large, persistent, and documented. Multiple strategy pairings.
- **Semi-exploitable** (LAW, TEC, SOC): Mixed participant base. Biases exist but are smaller and less reliable. Domain expertise provides moderate edge.
- **Unexploitable** (ECO, FIN): Professional participants suppress emotional biases within seconds. Speed is the only edge, and we can't compete with bots.
- **Theoretical** (SCI, ENV): Massive knowledge edge but no liquid markets to deploy it.
