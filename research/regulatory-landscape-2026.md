## 1. US Crypto Regulation

### 1.1 SEC Leadership: Paul Atkins Era

**SEC Chair**: Paul Atkins (confirmed 2025, pro-crypto Republican)

Atkins has fundamentally shifted the SEC from "regulation by enforcement" to a compliance-enabling, innovation-friendly posture. Key actions:

- **Digital assets declared top policy priority** at the agency
- **"Project Crypto" launched** — a joint SEC-CFTC initiative to modernize securities laws for blockchain-native assets, including frameworks for crypto asset classification, tokenized securities trading on AMMs, and custody guidance for non-security assets like stablecoins
- **First sitting SEC Chair to speak at Bitcoin Conference** (2026)
- **Self-custody described as a "foundational American value"**
- **Crypto in 401(k) accounts**: Atkins stated "the time is right to allow" crypto into retirement accounts, drawing pushback from Elizabeth Warren
- **Innovation Exemption Framework**: Planned publication granting crypto firms temporary regulatory relief to launch on-chain products without full securities-law compliance burden
- **Token Taxonomy**: On January 28, 2026, the SEC published a basic taxonomy of tokenized securities, clarifying that "securities, however represented, remain securities... economic reality trumps labels"

**Political tension**: House Democrats grilled Atkins on the enforcement pullback, probing why the agency slowed or dropped several high-profile crypto cases, including the Justin Sun/Tron case.

### 1.2 Major SEC Enforcement Case Outcomes

#### Coinbase — DISMISSED (February 2025)
- SEC filed joint stipulation to **dismiss** the civil enforcement action against Coinbase Inc. and Coinbase Global Inc.
- Original charges: operating as unregistered securities exchange, broker, and clearing agency; unregistered staking-as-a-service program
- **Outcome**: No fines. Coinbase retains existing business model and listings
- Rationale: "facilitate the Commission's ongoing efforts to reform and renew its regulatory approach to the crypto industry"

- SEC filed joint stipulation to dismiss **with prejudice** against Binance Holdings, BAM Trading, BAM Management, and Changpeng Zhao
- Original charges: illegally serving US users, inflating trading volumes, commingling customer funds
- **Outcome**: Case cannot be refiled. Separate $4.3B DOJ/FinCEN/CFTC settlement (from 2023) stands; CZ pled guilty, served prison time, stepped down but retained wealth
- Significance: "symbolic end to one of the most aggressive crypto crackdowns in U.S. history"

### 1.3 Stablecoin Legislation: GENIUS Act

**Status**: SIGNED INTO LAW (July 18, 2025)

- Passed Senate 68-30 (bipartisan), passed House July 17, 2025, signed by Trump July 18, 2025
- **First comprehensive federal stablecoin law**

**Key provisions**:
- Only "permitted issuers" may issue payment stablecoins for US persons
- 1:1 backing required (USD or low-risk assets)
- Payment stablecoins are explicitly **NOT securities** under federal securities laws and **NOT commodities** under the Commodity Exchange Act
- State-level issuers under $10B supervised by state regulators; above $10B by federal regulators

**Implementation timeline**:
- **July 18, 2026**: Regulators must promulgate final implementing regulations (1-year deadline)
- **January 2027**: Full enforcement of GENIUS Act provisions
- Treasury has issued an ANPRM (Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking) soliciting public comment on implementation

## 2. International Crypto Regulation

### 2.1 EU — MiCA (Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation)

**Status**: IN FORCE, FULL ENFORCEMENT JULY 1, 2026

MiCA timeline:
- June 2024: Stablecoin provisions took effect
- December 2024: Full regulation entered into force
- **July 1, 2026**: Grandfathering period expires — ALL CASPs must be fully compliant or face penalties

**Enforcement teeth**:
- Individuals: Fines up to EUR 5M or 3% of annual turnover
- Legal entities: Fines up to EUR 15M or 12.5% of annual turnover
- Over EUR 540M in penalties already issued since enforcement began
- Some member states (Finland, Latvia, Lithuania, Hungary, Netherlands, Poland, Slovenia) ended grandfathering early (6-month periods)

**CARF/DAC8**: Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework activated January 1, 2026 — CASPs must collect detailed user transaction data for tax reporting; first cross-border exchanges in 2027.

## 3. AI Regulation

### 3.1 EU AI Act

**Status**: IN FORCE; MAJOR ENFORCEMENT DATE AUGUST 2, 2026

#### Implementation Timeline

| Date | What Takes Effect |
|------|-------------------|
| **August 1, 2024** | AI Act entered into force |
| **February 2, 2025** | Prohibited AI practices banned (social scoring, exploitative manipulation, real-time biometric ID in public for law enforcement with limited exceptions) |
| **August 2, 2025** | GPAI model obligations; governance bodies established (AI Board, Scientific Panel, Advisory Forum) |
| **AUGUST 2, 2026** | **BULK OF OBLIGATIONS**: High-risk AI systems (Annex III), transparency obligations (Article 50), conformity assessment, market surveillance, deployer obligations |
| **August 2, 2027** | High-risk AI embedded in EU-harmonized products |

#### What August 2, 2026 Triggers
- **High-risk AI system requirements**: risk management, data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, transparency, human oversight, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity
- **Deployer obligations** for high-risk systems
- **Conformity assessment** procedures
- **Post-market monitoring** and incident reporting
- **Article 50 transparency**: disclosure of AI interactions, labeling of synthetic content, deepfake identification
- **Market surveillance framework** fully operational
- **Regulatory sandboxes**: At least one per member state required

#### Texas (Effective January 1, 2026)

**TRAIGA (Texas Responsible AI Governance Act, HB 149)**:
- Categorical restrictions on AI for manipulation, discrimination, CSAM, unlawful deepfakes, constitutional rights infringement
- Requires **discriminatory intent** (not mere disparate impact) for enforcement
- **First-in-nation AI regulatory sandbox** (36-month test period, no standard licenses needed)
- AG exclusive enforcement with 60-day cure period
- Preempts local AI ordinances

#### New York (Signed December 2025; Effective January 1, 2027)

**RAISE Act (Responsible AI Safety and Education)**:
- Targets developers with >$500M revenue developing frontier models trained with >10^26 FLOPS
- Must publish safety protocols covering "critical harm" (100+ deaths/injuries OR $1B+ damages)
- 72-hour incident reporting requirement
- Penalties: $10M per initial violation, $30M for subsequent

#### Colorado (Effective June 30, 2026 — postponed from Feb 1)

**Colorado AI Act** — first comprehensive state AI law:
- Deployers of high-risk AI must use "reasonable care" to avoid algorithmic discrimination
- Impact assessments, transparency disclosures, documentation of AI decision-making

**FEDERAL PREEMPTION THREAT**: Trump's December 2025 EO creates significant uncertainty for all state AI laws. The DOJ AI Litigation Task Force (operational since January 10, 2026) is empowered to challenge state laws in federal court. Commerce Secretary evaluation of "burdensome" state laws due March 11, 2026.

### 3.4 International AI Governance

#### UK — AI Security Institute (formerly AI Safety Institute)

- Renamed from "AI Safety Institute" to "AI Security Institute" in 2025, signaling strategic shift
- Positioning as **international convener** rather than domestic regulator
- Part of 10-jurisdiction International Network of AI Safety Institutes (Australia, Canada, EU, France, Japan, Kenya, South Korea, Singapore, UK, US)
- Helping set international evaluation standards — "gains influence over rules without legislating"

#### International AI Safety Report 2026

- Released **February 3, 2026**
- Led by Yoshua Bengio (Turing Award winner)
- Authored by 100+ international experts
- Backed by 30+ countries and organizations (EU, OECD, UN)
- Second iteration of the most comprehensive global AI risk assessment

#### China

- **Cybersecurity Law amendments** effective January 1, 2026 — first time AI governance written into foundational cybersecurity law (elevated from regulation to legislation)
- AI governance provisions: support AI innovation, promote training data resources and computing infrastructure, strengthen AI ethics regulation, enhance AI risk assessment
- Significantly strengthened penalties for data/cybersecurity violations
- Previous sector-specific rules: algorithms, deepfake tech, generative AI, AI labeling
- **No comprehensive AI law** (like EU AI Act); follows "agile, adaptive" approach similar to US

## Summary

The regulatory landscape as of February 2026 is defined by three converging forces:

1. **US deregulation/pro-innovation shift**: The Trump administration, SEC Chair Atkins, and a crypto-friendly Congress have created the most favorable US crypto environment ever. All major SEC enforcement cases (Coinbase, Binance, Ripple) are resolved. The GENIUS Act is law. Market structure legislation is advancing. The Strategic Bitcoin Reserve exists via EO.

2. **EU regulation-by-enforcement**: The EU is the global regulatory vanguard. MiCA full enforcement (July 1) and AI Act bulk enforcement (August 2) are the two largest regulatory events of 2026. Both will create significant compliance costs and potential enforcement actions.

3. **Federal-state tension on AI**: The US has no comprehensive federal AI law. States (California, Texas, New York, Colorado) have stepped in, but Trump's December 2025 EO and DOJ AI Litigation Task Force are actively working to preempt state laws. This creates enormous legal uncertainty for AI companies.

**For prediction markets specifically**: The CFTC is becoming a champion of prediction market legitimacy, but state gambling regulators remain hostile. The federal-state jurisdictional battle over prediction markets (Tennessee cease-and-desists, Nevada challenges) will be a defining story of 2026.
